02
§1 · The problem

A semester is scattered across forty decks and a notebook you stopped updating in week three.

Course material lives in a syllabus PDF, dozens of slide decks, assignment pages, and announcements. Lectures evaporate the moment they end. And the tools that fix this are proprietary, metered, and keep your recordings on someone else's servers.

The incumbent  UniFlow Study — $39.20/mo at the top tier. The free tier caps you at 30 AI conversations per month.
03
§2 · What we're building

Three pillars.

01 / ASK

Grounded answers

Every claim cited back to the exact page, file, or moment in a lecture. If your material doesn't support an answer, it says so instead of guessing.

02 / CAPTURE

Lectures, kept

At class time it makes the page, records, transcribes locally with Whisper, and puts a notes editor beside the transcript. Both get indexed.

03 / GENERATE

Study material

Mock tests, mindmaps, slides, flashcards — built from what's actually in your courses, each traceable to its source.

Note  Pillar 3 is the one UniFlow doesn't have. It's our wedge, and it came out of our own whiteboard session.

05
§4 · Decision one — the repositioning

From Canvas AI Tutor to SSB.

Was — Sprints 1–3

A Socratic tutor over one course

Guides rather than answers. One shared index per course, public to everyone enrolled. Scope: answer questions about course material.

→
Now — Sprints 4–9

A per-student second brain across all your courses

Ask, capture, generate. One private index per student. Scope: everything you'd otherwise keep in a notebook you abandoned.

  • A Socratic-only tutor is a feature, not a product. Nobody opens an app to be asked questions when they're behind.
  • Our own whiteboard named the real product. Transcribe, mock test, mindmaps, notes, flashcards — that was on the board, and none of it was in the spec.
  • Socratic survives as a toggle. Answer-first is the default. We keep the pedagogy without betting the product on it.

§5 · Decision two — the pivot

We built it on Onyx. It worked. We're dropping it anyway.

01 · LicenceFatal

The permissions are paid

Permission sync resolves through fetch_versioned_implementation to a commercially-licensed tier. An open-source product cannot have a permission model that needs a commercial licence. That's a contradiction, not a trade-off.

02 · Architecture

It's one shared corpus

Onyx is built as a single shared index with ACLs on top. We need one private index per student. That's either one Onyx per student — 11 containers each — or no enforcement at all.

03 · Deployment

Eleven containers

OpenSearch, Postgres, Redis, MinIO, two model servers, and more — 10–16 GB of Docker RAM. Our competitor is a desktop app. "Install Docker" is not a student onboarding flow.

Sprint 3 verdict — MODIFY  Keep RAG, change the substrate. It took three fixes just to survive a student token, and it still never reached modules or files — where most CMU course content actually lives. A documented negative result is worth the full 20% "Findings and Project Decision" weighting.

One index per student — physically

Not a filter on a shared store. A query-filter bug leaks someone else's lecture recordings; a missing directory just returns nothing. Worth the duplicated embeddings.

Personal data is never indexed

Grades, deadlines and submissions are read live from Canvas with your own token, per request. Always current, and never sitting in an embedding store.
